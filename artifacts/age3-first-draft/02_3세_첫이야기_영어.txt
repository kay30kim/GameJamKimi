AGE 3 — Where Is Toto's Hat?
English-first companion draft / For review only / Not connected to the game

Characters: Duri, a baby otter; Toto, a baby bear. Names and designs are provisional.
Core expression: “Can I help?” / “도와줄까?”
Setting: a flat grassy play area and a low, rounded bush.
Props: one red hat, one blue ball, and one red leaf.
The action, camera positions, and music cues follow the Korean draft. Spoken lines are written separately below.
Use the English version when English is selected; do not play both full scripts one after the other.
Estimated running time is roughly 2–3 minutes, excluding the child's choice. Time the final recording before production.

1. Say Hello to the Hat

Toto: “Duri, let's play ball!”
Duri: “Yes! Is your hat playing too?”
[Toto gently tips his hat, keeping his face visible.]
Toto: “Hello from my hat!”
Duri: “Hello, red hat!”
[They laugh. The ball rests on the grass in front of Duri.]

2. Whoosh! Where Did It Go?

[A little breeze lifts the hat. It lands behind the low bush, on flat ground.
The child sees a red corner for a moment before the characters notice it.]
Toto: “Oh! My hat!”
Toto: “Where did it go? I can't see it.”
[Duri looks at Toto, then at the ball.]
Duri: “Hmm... what shall I do?”

3. One Choice

A: “Help find the hat!”
B: “Roll the ball first!”
[Wait without a countdown. Play only the selected path.]

A. Help Now

Duri: “Toto, can I help?”
Toto: “Yes! Help me find my hat.”
Duri: “Let's look together!”
[Both walk to the front of the bush. The ball stays at their play spot.
The hat has not been found. Continue directly to scene 4.]

B. Play First, Then Listen

Duri: “I'll roll the ball once!”
[Duri rolls the ball a short distance, follows it, and picks it up.
Toto looks around in front of the bush.]
Toto: “I can't find my hat. I feel sad.”
[Show Toto's face. Duri stops and looks at him.]
Duri: “You still can't find it. Can I help?”
Toto: “Yes. Let's find it together.”
Duri: “Let's look!”
[Duri puts the ball back at their play spot and walks to Toto.
Both are now in front of the bush. Continue to scene 4 without replaying path A.]

4. Something Red!

[Both paths meet here: the friends are in front of the bush, the hat is still behind it,
and the ball is at their original play spot.]
Duri: “Red hat, where are you?”
[Duri spots a red leaf to the right.]
Duri: “Found it!”
[She picks up the leaf and holds it beside her face.]
Duri: “Oh! It's a leaf!”
Toto: “That's not my hat!”
[They laugh gently. Duri puts the leaf down.
The camera moves sideways to show a corner of the red hat. Give the child a moment to spot it.]
Toto: “Look! Something red!”
Duri: “Here it is! Your hat!”
[Duri walks around the side of the low bush, picks up the hat once, and gives it to Toto.
Toto receives it before speaking.]
Toto: “My hat!”

5. Two Happy Friends

[Toto puts on the hat. His eyes, nose, and mouth remain visible.]
Toto: “We found it! I'm happy. Thank you for helping!”
Duri: “You're smiling! That makes me happy too!”
[Duri turns slightly toward the viewer.]
Duri: “'도와줄까?' means 'Can I help?' in English.”
[Briefly show the two expressions together.]
Toto: “Duri, let's play ball!”
Duri: “Yes!”

6. A Little Speaking Moment

[Stay in the same place. Show Duri's face clearly.]
Duri: “Say it with me? 'Can I help?'”
Duri: “You can just listen, too.”
[Leave a short quiet space. Speaking is optional. No score or failure sound.
If the child chooses to keep waiting, continue only when they are ready.]

7. The Hat Can Play Too

[Both friends walk back to the ball. Toto still wears the hat. Duri picks up the ball.]
Toto: “Come on, hat. Let's play!”
Duri: “Here comes the ball!”
[Duri rolls the ball gently toward Toto. Keep both faces visible as the camera moves sideways.
Finish with the playful music returning. Do not reset the scene to its opening positions.]

Review notes
- This is the English-first version of the same story, not a sentence-by-sentence literal translation.
- Both paths show the chosen action before continuing. Helping changes the situation and both friends' feelings.
- Spoken text, captions, and audio must use this approved version together when production begins.
